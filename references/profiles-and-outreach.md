# Job-platform profiles and outreach

The rules here apply to every place a recruiter can see the candidate: professional networks (LinkedIn, Xing in German-speaking countries), job boards with candidate profiles (Indeed, StepStone, Glassdoor, Seek, Naukri and regional boards), company career portals where the candidate created an account, and freelance platforms if relevant. LinkedIn is used as the example where a platform-specific step is needed, because it is the most common one for professional roles.

Platform features and menu names change. When advising on a specific setting, check the platform's current help pages instead of relying on memory, and say so if it could not be checked.

## Collect the profiles

At intake, ask which platforms the candidate has a profile on, and for each one: the profile text (pasted, or the platform's export, for example LinkedIn's "More > Save to PDF"), the public profile link if there is one, and whether a CV file is stored there.

## Rules for every platform

- **Same facts everywhere.** Employers, titles and dates identical to the CV. If two versions of a title are in circulation, decide on one and use it everywhere.
- **Stored CV files.** Many boards and portals keep every CV ever uploaded and show one of them to employers by default. Check which file is visible or set as default; replace or delete versions that contradict the current CV.
- **Parsed profiles.** Many boards read the uploaded CV into structured fields. Parsing errors are common: merged jobs, wrong dates, garbled titles, skills lost. After every upload, review the parsed fields and correct them by hand.
- **Headline or target-title field.** Describe the band of roles the candidate is targeting, with two or three searchable terms, not only the last job title.
- **Summary or About.** The same story as the CV summary, in the platform's usual voice (first person on most networks).
- **Skills and keyword fields.** Recruiters search these. Fill them with the competencies the candidate is targeting, true ones only.
- **Location, relocation, work arrangement, notice period.** Match the candidate's real constraints.
- **Salary fields.** Some platforms show the expected salary to employers or use it to filter. Keep it consistent with what the candidate will say in interviews, or leave it empty where the field is optional.
- **Visibility.** Public, recruiters only, or hidden from the current employer: choose for the candidate's situation. For senior candidates, "recruiters only" signals are usually safer than public banners.
- **Freshness.** On some boards recently updated profiles appear higher in recruiter searches; check the board's help pages before promising this.

### Several applications on the same platforms

Recruiters at different companies may see the same profile. Every change must be true and must not contradict any CV already sent. When a change would help one application but conflict with another, list the conflict and let the user decide. Keep a note of which CV version went to which company (see the tracker in `after-applying.md`).

## LinkedIn-specific steps

- **Custom URL** (Edit public profile & URL): the name plus a short field keyword, for example `linkedin.com/in/first-last-scm` for supply chain, `-hr`, `-fin`, `-ux`. It is easier to read on a CV, signals the field, and avoids the default random digits.
- **Before editing**, turn off "Share profile updates with your network", so a series of edits does not broadcast to former colleagues.
- **Open to Work**: "Recruiters only" is usually safer than the public banner for senior roles.
- **Photo and banner**: present and professional.

## Profile link in the CV

Put one stable, clean link to the candidate's main professional profile in the CV contact line: on LinkedIn the custom URL; on another platform its public profile link if it offers one; otherwise leave it out. Check the link opens while logged out.

## Message to the recruiter or job poster

Use when the platform shows who posted the job or lets candidates message the recruiter. If it does not, look up the recruiter or hiring manager on a professional network or the company's careers page.

Rules:
- Language of the company and the posting.
- Human, not formal. The reader is a person who reads many identical messages.
- Short: under 120 words; many platforms cap message length.
- One concrete reason for interest (something specific about the company, the role or the problem), not adjectives like "excited" or "passionate".
- The candidate's single closest proof, with their real title if it maps directly to the posting.
- If there is a hard constraint (location, relocation), raise it at the end and leave a door open ("is this a firm requirement, or are there roles where it matters less?").
- Do not attach the CV unasked. Offer it: "I can share my CV if useful." Asking first gives a second contact point and lets the candidate send the right version.
- One message. No chasing on the same channel.

Template (adapt, do not paste as is):

> Hello [Name], thank you for connecting. I saw the [Role] opening. I have [N] years in [field]; at [Company] I [closest proof with scale]. What draws me to this role is [specific reason]. [Optional constraint question.] I can share my CV if useful.

## Messages to former colleagues and managers

Senior roles are often filled before or without a posting. People who worked with the candidate hear about them first and can introduce them past the screening filter.

Who: five to ten people who know the candidate's work and now hold director-level roles or sit close to hiring decisions. Former managers first. Use whatever channel the candidate already uses with each person.

Rules:
- Personal, not a broadcast. A public post made months ago does not count: people forget, or assume the candidate found something.
- Do not ask for a job. Tell them what happened, what the candidate is looking for (the band, not one title), and ask them to keep it in mind.
- Make saying yes easy: offer a coffee or a short call, nothing more.

Template:

> Hi [Name], it has been a while, I hope you are well. [One line on what happened: role ended / company closed / project finished.] I am now looking at [band of roles, e.g. country-level logistics or supply chain director roles]. If you hear of something in your circle, I would be grateful if you kept me in mind. I would also be glad to catch up over coffee.

## When the candidate has an insider connection to the company

Ask the contact for their view before applying, rather than asking for the job. Raise the obvious question (level, relocation, career gap) yourself. Send documents only after they say yes. Then apply through the normal channel as well, so the application exists in the system.
