# CV and cover letter rules

## CV

### Structure (top to bottom)
1. Name.
2. One contact line: city and country, phone, one professional profile link (for example a custom LinkedIn URL), email. Not in the page header: some ATS ignore headers.
3. **Target line**: the role title from the posting plus two or three specialisms it asks for, in its wording, where true.
4. **Summary**: three to five sentences. Who the candidate is and for how long, the scale handled, two or three results with numbers, the posting's key skills where true.
5. **Core competencies**: 12 to 20 short items separated by bullets. Mirror the posting's terms that are true. Order by relevance to this posting.
6. **Experience**, most recent first. Job header: company and title on the left in bold, dates and location on the right. Three to eight bullets for recent roles, one line for roles older than about 15 years.
7. Education, certifications, technical skills, languages, an optional "Additional" line.

### Bullets
- Formula: **action verb + what + scale + result**, with the method where it is interesting. "Cut picking errors by 40% across two warehouses by redesigning slotting around order frequency."
- Most relevant bullet first within each role, measured against this posting, not against the candidate's pride.
- One idea per bullet. Two lines maximum.
- Numbers: use the candidate's. Same format throughout (one decimal style, one currency style, "15,000 m²" not "15.000m²" in English).
- Prefer "built", "set up", "redesigned" only when true (Rule 3). "Ran", "managed", "improved" are honest and strong enough.

### Tailoring for a posting
- Rewrite the target line and summary.
- Reorder competencies and bullets.
- Add posting vocabulary only where it describes something real.
- Shorten what is irrelevant to this posting rather than deleting it entirely, unless the CV is over two pages.
- Keep one version per application, named clearly (for example `First_Last_CV_Company_Role.docx`). Never send a file named after another company.

### Titles
Use the real title. If it undersells or oversells the job, keep it and let the bullets show the scope. Whatever is chosen must match every job-platform profile and every other open application (Rule 5). A later reference check will compare them.

### ATS safety
- Single column. No tables, text boxes, images or icons for content.
- Standard section names (Experience, Education, Skills).
- Contact details in the body, not the header or footer.
- Plain bullet characters; no box-drawing or private-use symbols.
- Dates in one consistent format.
- Send PDF unless the system asks for Word; export with Word's own "Save as PDF".

## Cover letter

### Shape
- One page, 350 to 450 words, same font and name header as the CV.
- Date, the role and company on one bold line, "Dear Hiring Team" (or the person's name if known).

### Content, in order
1. **Opening (one or two sentences)**: the employer's problem, or the candidate's closest proof that they have solved it. Not "I am writing to apply". Not a slogan.
2. **Two or three proof paragraphs**: each one a specific story from the candidate's real work, with a number where they gave one, linked to a requirement in the posting.
3. **The biggest gap, named once**: what is missing, and what the candidate brings instead. Two or three sentences, no apology.
4. **Questions answered before they are asked**: career gap, relocation or commute, level (why this role at this point), availability. One or two sentences each.
5. **Close**: one sentence inviting a conversation. No pleading, no "I would be honoured".

### Language to avoid
These read as generic or machine-written. `scripts/docx_check.py` flags most of them.
- Stock phrases: "excited to apply", "passionate about", "proven track record", "results-driven", "leverage", "seamless", "robust", "dynamic", "synergy", "perfect fit", "I believe my skills align", "team player", "detail-oriented".
- Em dashes. Use commas, colons or full stops.
- Repeated "not X, but Y" / "rather than" contrasts and "the hard part was" framing. One is fine; three is a pattern.
- Aphorism openings ("Great logistics is invisible.").
- Adjectives doing the work of evidence ("highly skilled", "exceptional").

### Voice check
After drafting, read each paragraph and ask: would the candidate say this sentence in an interview? If not, rewrite it in plainer words. Then have the user read the letter aloud once.

## Re-approaching after a rejection

Only when something has changed (for example the posting was reposted) and the stated reason is addressable. One short message, at most once:
- thank them for the feedback;
- say you are not asking them to reverse the decision;
- answer the hidden concerns directly: cost (willing to work within their band), commitment (not a short stop), working style (learns the existing system before improving it);
- close by saying you will not write again if they decline.
