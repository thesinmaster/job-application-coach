# Fit report

Write it in the user's language. Keep it to one screen where possible. Lead with the verdict.

## Structure

**1. Verdict (one paragraph).** How strong the fit is, the single biggest strength, the single biggest risk, and the recommendation: apply fully, apply light, or do not apply.

**2. Requirement table.** One row per requirement in the posting, in the posting's order.

| Posting asks for | Candidate has | Match |
|---|---|---|
| exact requirement, shortened | the specific evidence, with a number where the candidate gave one | Strong / Partial / Bridge / Gap |

"Bridge" means related real experience exists and can be described in the posting's vocabulary. "Gap" means nothing related exists.

**3. Strengths.** Two to four, each tied to evidence. Include strengths the posting does not ask for but that would clearly help (a story from the intake that never made the CV is often the best one).

**4. Gaps.** Only the real ones. For each: how much it matters to this posting, and whether to bridge, name or prepare (SKILL.md Stage 3).

**5. Risks.** Anything outside skills that could stop the application:
- location, commute, relocation, travel, on-site requirement;
- level (see below);
- sector distance;
- tenure pattern: many short stays, one very long stay, recent gap;
- language requirement;
- salary band (if inferable);
- title mismatch between headline, body and emails.

**6. Recommendation and options.** The three choices with your pick and the reason.

## Reading the level correctly

Over-qualification is the most common silent rejection for experienced candidates. Check:
- **Title gap**: candidate's last two titles against the posting's title. Two or more levels apart in either direction is a risk.
- **Scale gap**: people, sites, budget. If the candidate ran ten times what the role covers, expect "more experience than this role requires".
- **The real job**: when the headline and body disagree (for example a body describing a country leadership role under a "support manager" headline), the smaller description is usually the real scope and the real salary band.
- **Salary band**: if the band is likely far below the candidate's floor, say so before any work is done.

Under-qualification signs are the mirror image: required years not met, scale an order of magnitude smaller, no experience leading through other managers when the role requires it.

When the level is off, say so plainly and suggest the band of roles that fits better. Do not "fix" over-qualification by hiding real experience; fix it by choosing better targets, or by answering the hidden concerns directly (cost, how long the candidate will stay, whether they will accept the existing way of working).

## Tone

Honest, specific, short. No reassurance that is not earned. If the candidate is a long shot, say so and say why.
