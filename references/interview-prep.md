# Interview preparation

## Method

1. Read the CV and cover letter as the recruiter for this role would. Note every place they will pause: a gap, a short stay, a title that does not fit, a jump in level, a claim that needs proof.
2. List the questions in the order they are likely to come.
3. Draft each answer from the candidate's real stories (intake notes). Structure: situation, what they did, result, what they learned. 75 to 90 seconds spoken; under two minutes always.
4. Spread the stories: do not use the same example for three questions. Map each question to a different story.
5. Give the candidate the outline, not a script. They should put it in their own words and practise aloud.

## Question bank

Always prepare:
- **Tell me about yourself.** The thread through the career in one sentence, then three roles in one line each, then why this role.
- **Why this company / this role?** Something specific to the company, plus a link to the candidate's experience.
- **Why did you leave / what have you been doing since?** Factual, short, no blame. For a gap: what they did with the time and that it is finished.
- **Biggest achievement.**
- **Biggest challenge.**
- **Biggest weakness.** A real behaviour, its cost, and what the candidate does about it now. End with "it is not fully solved" rather than claiming it is fixed. Avoid disguised strengths ("I am a perfectionist").
- **A stakeholder who disagreed with your recommendation.** Must be someone the candidate kept working with (a peer, a manager, another department), not an outside adversary. The best endings are not "I won" but "the decision stopped being reopened".
- **A negotiation.**
- **Leading or changing a team.**
- **The gaps from the fit report**: the honest answer and what the candidate has done to close them.
- **Salary expectation and notice period** (see below).

Prepare when the CV shows it:
- **Frequent moves / short tenures (under about three years).** Explain the pattern once (project-based roles, company closures, a deliberate move), show what was finished in each, and state what the candidate is looking for now that makes a longer stay likely.
- **Very long tenure in one place.** Show range inside it: different scopes, systems, managers, crises.
- **Over-qualification.** Answer the three hidden concerns: cost, how long they will stay, whether they will accept the existing way of working. Never answer with "I just want a job".
- **Career change or sector change.** What transfers, with one example; what is new and how they are learning it.
- **Role-specific technical questions.** Ask the user for the domain if needed and list the five most likely technical questions with short model answers grounded in the candidate's experience.

## Questions the candidate should ask

- How big is the team, and who reports to this role?
- Which budget does the role own?
- Who does this role report to?
- Why is the role open: new, replacement, restructuring?
- What would a strong first six months look like?
- When the headline and body of the posting differed: "The posting describes [X]; could you tell me how the role sits in the organisation?"

## One-way video interviews

Platforms show a written question, a short preparation time and a limited recording time, sometimes with few or no retakes.
- Do the platform's test recording first and check the retake rules.
- Phone camera, horizontal, at eye level, light on the face, no window behind, plain background.
- Look at the camera, not the screen.
- 75 to 90 seconds per answer; never read. Three or four keywords taped behind the camera.
- Start the first answer with the name and a smile.
- In written fields on the same platform, answer with specific tools and results the candidate really used; never name a tool they have not used just because the question lists it as an example.

## Salary expectation

1. **Research** the local market for the role and level with web search: job sites' salary pages, published surveys, recruiter reports. Note the source, the date and whether figures are gross or net, expectations or actual pay. Say clearly how reliable the data is.
2. **Gross, monthly or annual as the country's custom.** In countries with progressive tax, a net agreement changes value during the year; employers negotiate gross.
3. **Employee or contractor.** A contractor rate must cover tax, social security, equipment and unpaid time off; it is not comparable to a salary. Give the main facts and recommend a local accountant before signing.
4. **Give one number, not a range**, with a short reason tied to the scope. Keep a private floor and a walk-away point. Optionally add "I would like to understand the full package (bonus, car, health insurance)".
5. **After giving the number, stop.** Do not soften it in the next sentence.
6. **Do not state previous salary** unless legally required; price against the role's scope.
7. If the candidate writes that they will "negotiate within the company's range", they must be ready to accept a figure inside that range later.
